Five visual directions for the Monday match app

Each direction dresses the same three real screens: the group dashboard in a browser, and the match signup and lineup on a phone. Palette, type and shape change; the product does not. Names are proposed separately at the end and pair with any direction.

The brief

One app, two interfaces

The browser is where the admin runs the group on Sunday morning. The phone is where fourteen people tap “Anotarme” from WhatsApp at 12:01. They need different shells, not different apps.

Browser: the admin desk

  • Persistent sidebar with the group switcher and sections: Partidos, Jugadores, Estadísticas, Reglas, Ajustes.
  • Two-column pages. The match on the left, the table and upcoming dates on the right. Nothing hides behind a tap.
  • Dense tools. Drag-and-drop lineup editor, pair rules, guest management, image export, all with room to breathe.
  • Layout breakpoint around 900px. Tablets in landscape get the desk; portrait gets the pocket.

Mobile: the player's pocket

  • One column, one job. The screen opens on the next match with the count of spots and a single sticky action in thumb reach: Anotarme, or Bajarme once you are in.
  • Bottom tab bar with three stops: Partido, Grupo, Yo. No hamburger.
  • Built for the WhatsApp round trip. Deep links land on the match, the lineup exports as an image, the app installs to the home screen as a PWA.
  • Admin actions stay available but move to a sheet, not the main flow.
Shared

Same design tokens, same components, same routes. The current code already drives colors through CSS variables and a dark class, so any direction below is a token and font swap plus a responsive shell. What does not exist yet is the shell itself: today there is a single top header on every screen size.

Directions

Five ways to dress it

Letters are for pointing at them in conversation, not a ranking. Each mockup uses a working name so the wordmark can be judged in context; swap freely.

Direction A

Nocturno

Floodlit five-a-side at 9 pm. Near-black green ground, one chalk-lime accent, wide uppercase headlines. The closest evolution of the current dark theme, with a sharper voice.

Mood

Synthetic turf under lights. Serious about the game, casual about everything else.

Why it works

Keeps the dark-first default players already know. Lime reads instantly on a phone outdoors and as a WhatsApp preview.

Watch out

Expanded uppercase type needs discipline: headlines and the wordmark only, never body copy. Light mode should be a warm grey, not white.

Direction B

Club de barrio

The neighbourhood club that has existed since 1954. Off-white ground, navy and burgundy from the jersey, a warm serif for names and condensed numerals like the ones on a shirt back.

Mood

Crest on the door, trophies behind the bar. Belonging first, software second.

Why it works

The group has a history and a Monday ritual; this direction treats it like a club, which makes stats and MVPs feel earned rather than gamified.

Watch out

Light-first, so the dark mode needs its own navy ground rather than an inversion. Serif at small sizes on phones needs the 600 weight.

Direction C

Pizarra

The coach's chalkboard. Slate-green ground with chalk-white dashed lines, cone-orange for the one thing to do, a geometric display face and monospaced numbers. Puts the team generator at the centre of the brand.

Mood

Halftime talk. Tactical, a bit nerdy, deliberately un-corporate.

Why it works

The AI lineup is the feature nobody else's WhatsApp group has. A board-and-chalk language makes the lineup screen the hero and the exported image instantly recognisable.

Watch out

Dashed borders everywhere get noisy; reserve them for cards and the pitch, keep buttons solid. Orange on green needs the dark ink label, never white.

Direction D

Domingo

Sunday-league pop. Warm white ground, tangerine for action, cobalt for the second team, pill buttons and big friendly counts. The lightest, most consumer-app direction of the five.

Mood

Sunny, loud, easy. The app you open while still in bed on Sunday.

Why it works

Lowest friction for guests who arrive from an invite link and have never seen the app. Big tap targets, unambiguous primary action, colour that survives a cracked screen.

Watch out

Tangerine and cobalt together can tip into “startup”; keep cobalt for team Claras and links only. Dark mode should be a deep aubergine, not black.

Direction E

Diario

The Tuesday sports page. Paper white, ink black, one red for the call to action. Condensed uppercase headlines over a reading serif, rules instead of boxes. Stats and results become the story.

Mood

Match report. Dry, confident, a little theatrical about a 7-a-side.

Why it works

The app is about to gain crowd-sourced results, goal scorers and MVPs. A newspaper voice gives all of that a natural home and makes the weekly recap shareable as a “front page”.

Watch out

Hairlines and serif need generous spacing on phones or it turns into a spreadsheet. Dark mode is an inverted masthead, not a grey theme.

Names

What to call it

The strongest names come from the ritual itself: the words the group already types on Sunday. “fulbot” has served, but the “bot” suffix promises a chatbot and the app is a list, a pitch and a coach.

Shortlist · 1PicadoThe Rioplatense word for a pick-up match. Two syllables, no accent, describes the product to anyone who would use it. Feels like a brand, not a feature.
Shortlist · 2Falta UnoThe eternal WhatsApp message. Funny, memorable, and it names the exact moment the app exists to solve. Reads great in a notification.
Shortlist · 3CanchaThe pitch. Neutral, travels across every Spanish-speaking country, ages well. The safe choice if you ever go beyond one city.

Before committing

Check the .app and .com.ar domains, search the App Store and Play Store even though it ships as a PWA today, paste the name into a WhatsApp message and look at the link preview, and say it out loud to someone from Spain or Mexico. Avoid accents in the name itself so the URL and the wordmark match.

Next steps

How this becomes code

  1. Pick a direction and a name, or ask for a hybrid (for example B's palette with A's dark-first default).
  2. Token swap. The palette lands in the existing CSS variables in the global stylesheet, light and dark, and the fonts replace Inter in the root layout.
  3. Responsive shell. A sidebar layout for the dashboard group above 900px and a bottom tab bar plus sticky action below it. The header component becomes the mobile top bar.
  4. Assets. App icon, manifest colours, WhatsApp share image template for the lineup export, and the invite page as the first thing a guest sees in the new brand.
